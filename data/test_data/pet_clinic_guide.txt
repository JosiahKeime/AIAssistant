## Vaccination Schedules

### Puppies (Dogs)

| Age | Vaccines |
|---|---|
| 6-8 weeks | DHPP (Distemper, Hepatitis, Parainfluenza, Parvovirus) — 1st dose |
| 10-12 weeks | DHPP — 2nd dose; Leptospirosis — 1st dose; Bordetella (kennel cough) |
| 14-16 weeks | DHPP — 3rd dose; Leptospirosis — 2nd dose; Rabies (required by NC law) |
| 12-16 months | DHPP booster; Rabies booster; Leptospirosis annual |
| Annually thereafter | DHPP (every 1-3 years depending on vaccine type); Rabies (every 3 years after initial booster); Leptospirosis (annually); Bordetella (annually if boarding/grooming); Canine Influenza (recommended, 2-dose series) |

### Kittens (Cats)

| Age | Vaccines |
|---|---|
| 6-8 weeks | FVRCP (Feline Viral Rhinotracheitis, Calicivirus, Panleukopenia) — 1st dose |
| 10-12 weeks | FVRCP — 2nd dose; FeLV (Feline Leukemia) — 1st dose (if going outdoors) |
| 14-16 weeks | FVRCP — 3rd dose; FeLV — 2nd dose; Rabies (required by NC law) |
| 12-16 months | FVRCP booster; Rabies booster; FeLV booster (if outdoor) |

## Spay/Neuter Information

Mountainview Pet Clinic recommends spaying or neutering pets between 4 and 6 months of age for cats and small-breed dogs, and between 12 and 18 months for large-breed dogs (over 50 lbs).

### Procedure Costs (as of 2024)

- Dog spay (under 50 lbs): $350 – $450
- Dog spay (over 50 lbs): $450 – $600

- Dog neuter (over 50 lbs): $375 – $500

All procedures include pre-surgical bloodwork, anesthesia, pain management, and a post-op recheck at 10-14 days.

## Common Toxic Foods for Pets

**Dangerous for Dogs AND Cats:**
- Chocolate (especially dark chocolate and baking chocolate) — contains theobromine
- Grapes and raisins — can cause acute kidney failure
- Onions and garlic — damage red blood cells
- Xylitol (artificial sweetener, found in sugar-free gum, some peanut butters) — causes rapid insulin release in dogs
- Macadamia nuts — cause weakness, vomiting, tremors in dogs
- Alcohol — even small amounts are dangerous

**Dangerous Specifically for Cats:**
- Lilies (all parts of the plant) — highly toxic, can cause fatal kidney failure
- Raw fish (in large quantities) — contains thiaminase which destroys vitamin B1

**If your pet ingests any of these substances, call our office immediately or contact the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center at (888) 426-4435 (a $75 consultation fee applies).**

## Dental Care

Dental disease affects over 80% of dogs and 70% of cats by age 3. Mountainview Pet Clinic offers comprehensive dental services:

- **Annual dental exam:** Included with wellness visit (no extra charge)
- **Professional dental cleaning:** $350 – $600 (includes anesthesia, scaling, polishing, and dental X-rays)
- **Tooth extraction:** $75 – $300 per tooth depending on complexity

Signs your pet may need dental care:
- Bad breath (halitosis)
- Red, swollen, or bleeding gums
- Difficulty eating or dropping food
- Pawing at the mouth
- Loose or discolored teeth

### Home Dental Care Tips
- Brush your pet's teeth daily with pet-specific toothpaste (NEVER use human toothpaste — fluoride is toxic to pets)
- Dental chews approved by the Veterinary Oral Health Council (VOHC) can help reduce plaque
- Add a water additive to your pet's bowl for additional antibacterial benefits

## Emergency Symptoms — When to Seek Immediate Care

Contact our office or the emergency clinic immediately if your pet shows:
- Difficulty breathing or persistent coughing
- Collapse or inability to stand
- Seizures lasting more than 2 minutes
- Uncontrolled bleeding
- Suspected broken bones
- Bloated abdomen with unproductive retching (especially in large-breed dogs — may indicate GDV/bloat)
- Ingestion of a toxic substance
- Not eating or drinking for more than 24 hours
- Straining to urinate (especially male cats — can indicate life-threatening urethral obstruction)

# History of the Internet — Key Milestones

## 1960s: The Foundations

### 1962 — The Concept
J.C.R. Licklider of MIT published memos describing his vision of a "Galactic Network" — a globally interconnected set of computers through which anyone could access data and programs from any site. Licklider became the first head of the computer research program at DARPA (then called ARPA) in October 1962.

### 1965 — First Wide-Area Computer Network
Lawrence Roberts and Thomas Marill connected the TX-2 computer at MIT Lincoln Laboratory to the Q-32 computer at System Development Corporation in Santa Monica, California via a low-speed dial-up telephone line. This was the first (however small) wide-area computer network ever built.

### 1969 — ARPANET Goes Live
On October 29, 1969, the first message was sent over ARPANET from UCLA to the Stanford Research Institute (SRI). The intended message was "LOGIN" but the system crashed after transmitting just the first two letters: "LO." The full "LOGIN" message was successfully sent about an hour later. By the end of 1969, four nodes were connected: UCLA, SRI, UC Santa Barbara, and the University of Utah.

## 1970s: Protocols and Email

### 1971 — First Email
Ray Tomlinson, a programmer at BBN Technologies, sent the first email between two computers on ARPANET. He chose the "@" symbol to separate the user name from the computer name (e.g., tomlinson@bbn-tenexa). Tomlinson later said the first email was something forgettable like "QWERTYUIOP."

### 1973 — TCP/IP Proposed
Vint Cerf and Bob Kahn published a paper proposing the Transmission Control Protocol (TCP), which later split into TCP and IP (Internet Protocol). This became the foundational protocol suite of the internet. The design solved the problem of connecting diverse, incompatible networks into a single "network of networks."

### 1974 — The Word "Internet"
The term "internet" was first used in a 1974 RFC (Request for Comments) document by Vint Cerf, Yogen Dalal, and Carl Sunshine as a shorthand for "internetworking."

### 1978 — First Spam Email
Gary Thuerk, a marketing manager at Digital Equipment Corporation (DEC), sent the first unsolicited mass email to approximately 393 ARPANET users on May 3, 1978, advertising DEC computer systems. The email generated complaints but also reportedly resulted in $13 million in sales.

## 1980s: Growth and the DNS

### 1983 — ARPANET Switches to TCP/IP
On January 1, 1983 — known as "flag day" — ARPANET officially switched from the older NCP (Network Control Protocol) to TCP/IP. This date is often considered the true "birthday" of the internet as we know it.

### 1983 — Domain Name System (DNS)
Paul Mockapetris invented the Domain Name System (DNS), which translates human-readable domain names (like example.com) into IP addresses. Before DNS, users had to remember numerical addresses.

### 1985 — First Registered Domain Name
The first .com domain name, symbolics.com, was registered on March 15, 1985 by Symbolics Inc., a computer manufacturer in Cambridge, Massachusetts.

### 1988 — First Major Internet Worm
The "Morris Worm," created by Robert Tappan Morris (then a Cornell University graduate student), infected approximately 6,000 computers — about 10% of the internet at the time. Morris became the first person convicted under the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act. He was sentenced to 3 years probation, 400 hours of community service, and a $10,050 fine.

## 1990s: The World Wide Web

### 1989 — Tim Berners-Lee Proposes the Web
Tim Berners-Lee, a British scientist at CERN in Geneva, Switzerland, submitted a proposal titled "Information Management: A Proposal" in March 1989. This paper laid out the concepts of HTML, URLs, and HTTP — the three technologies that form the foundation of the World Wide Web.

### 1991 — First Website
The first-ever website (info.cern.ch) went live on August 6, 1991. It explained what the World Wide Web was and how to use a browser.

### 1993 — Mosaic Browser
The Mosaic web browser, developed at the National Center for Supercomputing Applications (NCSA) at the University of Illinois, was released in April 1993. It was the first browser to display images inline with text (rather than in separate windows), making the web visually appealing to non-technical users. The team, led by Marc Andreessen, later founded Netscape.

### 1995 — Commercialization Boom
Several landmark companies launched or went public in 1995:
- Amazon.com launched on July 16, 1995, originally as an online bookstore
- eBay was founded on September 3, 1995, by Pierre Omidyar
- The Netscape IPO on August 9, 1995, kicked off the dot-com boom — shares were priced at $28 and closed at $75 on the first day

### 1998 — Google Founded
Larry Page and Sergey Brin, PhD students at Stanford University, incorporated Google on September 4, 1998. Their search engine was distinguished by the PageRank algorithm, which ranked web pages based on the number and quality of links pointing to them.

## 2000s: Social and Mobile

### 2001 — Wikipedia Launched
Jimmy Wales and Larry Sanger launched Wikipedia on January 15, 2001. It became the largest encyclopedia ever created, with over 6.8 million English-language articles as of 2024.

### 2004 — Facebook Launched
Mark Zuckerberg launched "TheFacebook" from his Harvard dorm room on February 4, 2004. It was initially limited to Harvard students before expanding to other universities and eventually the general public in September 2006.

### 2005 — YouTube Launched
YouTube was founded by three former PayPal employees — Chad Hurley, Steve Chen, and Jawed Karim. The first video, "Me at the zoo," was uploaded by Karim on April 23, 2005. Google acquired YouTube in October 2006 for $1.65 billion.

### 2007 — The iPhone
Apple released the first iPhone on June 29, 2007. While not the first smartphone, the iPhone's multi-touch interface and mobile Safari browser transformed how people accessed the internet, accelerating the shift to mobile-first design.

## 2010s–2020s: The Modern Internet

- **2010:** Instagram launched on October 6, gaining 1 million users in 2 months
- **2011:** Over 2 billion people were using the internet worldwide
- **2016:** Google announced that over 50% of all searches were coming from mobile devices
- **2020:** COVID-19 pandemic accelerated internet usage; global internet traffic surged approximately 40% during lockdowns
- **2022:** Over 5 billion people (approximately 63% of the world's population) were using the internet
- **2023:** Generative AI tools (ChatGPT, Midjourney, etc.) became mainstream consumer products, with ChatGPT reaching 100 million monthly active users within 2 months of launch — the fastest-growing consumer application in history
